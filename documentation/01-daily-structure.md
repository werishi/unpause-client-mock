# Daily Structure and Routines

> Covers README section: 1. Structure your day, week and month

## Summary

Physical activity is among the better-supported tools for depression. Light activity such as walking or yoga still helps, but most trials are small and the certainty is low. If-then plans and stable daily routines help people start and repeat behaviours. Regular sleep-wake timing, daylight and treating insomnia are all linked to better mood, though much of the rhythm evidence is observational. For Unpause, this supports small, structured, repeatable plans tied to fixed times of day and to the user's values. The evidence is much thinner for the specific mechanics: the daily energy check-in, the two-minute version and the values compass have not been tested as such.

## Key findings

### Templates and graded physical activity

- Exercise works as a treatment for depression. A network meta-analysis of 218 RCTs (14,170 participants) found moderate effects compared with active controls for walking or jogging (g = -0.62), yoga (g = -0.55) and strength training (g = -0.49). Certainty was low for walking or jogging and very low for all other types ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)).
- Light activity still counts. In the same review, light activity such as walking or hatha yoga had a clinically meaningful effect (g = -0.58). Vigorous activity looked somewhat stronger (g = -0.74), but the intervals overlapped widely. The effect of total weekly dose was unclear ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)).
- Yoga and strength training had lower dropout than active controls. Trials that gave participants more autonomy showed weaker effects, which surprised the authors. This is a study-level association and may be confounded ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)).
- A meta-analysis of 41 RCTs (2,264 participants) against non-active controls found a large effect overall. Restricted to low risk-of-bias trials, the effect was moderate (SMD = -0.67, number needed to treat about 2.8). Effects were large in supervised programmes, and the authors recommend supervised, group-based, moderate-intensity aerobic exercise. Many trials were small and methods varied a lot ([Heissel et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106282)).
- Low doses matter most for prevention. In 15 prospective cohorts (191,130 adults), half the recommended activity volume was linked to about 18% lower risk of later depression, and the full recommended volume to about 25% lower risk. Extra benefit above that was small. These are observational data in general populations, not treatment trials ([Pearce et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0609)).
- People with depression mostly stay in exercise trials. Pooled dropout was about 18% in 40 RCTs, and lower than in control groups. Higher baseline depression predicted more dropout. Programmes supervised by exercise professionals had less dropout ([Stubbs et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.10.019)).
- The NICE guideline lists group exercise and behavioural activation among the first-line treatment options for less severe depression ([NICE, 2022](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)).

### Energy check-in

- Fatigue and low energy are reported in more than 90% of people with major depressive disorder. The review notes that studies measure fatigue in many different ways. It focuses on medication but also discusses exercise and light therapy ([Ghanean et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-018-0490-z)).
- People with depression or anhedonia are less willing to spend effort to get rewards. This is clearer for physical effort than for mental effort. The authors link this directly to behavioural activation ([Horne et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102065)).
- In daily life, activity and mood feed each other. In a two-week study with five daily mood prompts and activity trackers (359 people), more physical activity predicted better mood over the next hours, and better mood predicted more activity. The link between activity and lower negative mood was stronger in people with current depression or anxiety. It varied a lot from person to person ([Difrancesco et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.23298)).
- No study found here tested a daily self-rated energy check-in that changes the day's plan. The design is a reasonable inference from the findings above, not a tested intervention.

### Two-minute version and habit formation

- If-then plans ("When X happens, I will do Y") had a medium-to-large effect on reaching goals (d = 0.65) across 94 tests. One effect was helping people get started on a goal ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)).
- In people with mental health problems (clinical samples and people above clinical cut-offs), if-then plans had a large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.99, 28 tests, 1,636 participants). The effect held across different problems and goals ([Toli et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjc.12086)).
- Habits form gradually and at very different speeds. Participants repeated one behaviour daily in the same context (for example "after breakfast"). Reaching 95% of maximum automaticity took 18 to 254 days. Missing a single day did not materially harm habit formation ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- A 2024 review (20 studies, 2,601 participants) found medians of 59 to 66 days to form a health habit, with individual times from 4 to 335 days. Morning practice built stronger habits than evening practice in one study. Self-chosen habits were stronger than assigned ones. Most studies had a high risk of bias, and none were in people with depression ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)).
- No study found here tested a "two-minute version" of tasks in depression. The support is indirect: lower effort costs ([Horne et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102065)), if-then plans for starting ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)) and simple, repeated behaviours for habits ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)).

### Daily rhythm anchors

- Weaker day-night activity rhythms go with mood problems. In 91,105 UK Biobank participants who wore wrist trackers for 7 days, each quintile lower in rhythm strength was linked to higher odds of lifetime major depression (OR 1.06), lower happiness and more loneliness. The study is cross-sectional, so cause and effect are unclear ([Lyall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(18)30139-1)).
- Irregular sleep timing is linked to depressive symptoms. In 2,115 first-year doctors tracked with wearables, more night-to-night variation in sleep duration and wake time went with more depressive symptoms. Within each person, smaller day-to-day shifts in wake time predicted better next-day mood. Effects were small, and regularity mattered about as much as average sleep length ([Fang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00400-z)).
- Time outdoors in daylight is linked to better mood. In about 400,000 UK Biobank adults, each extra hour outdoors per day was linked to lower odds of lifetime depression, less low mood, easier waking and fewer insomnia symptoms. The data are self-reported and observational. Longitudinal effects were smaller than cross-sectional ones ([Burns et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.08.056)).
- Bright light therapy helps in non-seasonal depression as an add-on treatment. Across 11 RCTs (858 patients), remission was 40.7% with light versus 23.5% without (OR 2.42). Most trials used a 10,000 lux light box for 30 to 60 minutes a day, some in the early morning. The review did not analyse side effects, and four trials included people with bipolar disorder without separate results ([Menegaz de Almeida et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.2871)).
- Digital CBT for insomnia also lowers depressive symptoms. Across 22 RCTs (10,486 participants), the effect on depression was small to moderate (SMD = -0.42) and on insomnia large (SMD = -0.76). Fully automated programmes still worked. Effects were larger when more users completed the programme. In the 12 studies that reported it, only about 60% completed all sessions ([Lee et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00800-3)).
- Treating insomnia may prevent depression. In 291 older adults with insomnia, CBT-I halved new or recurrent major depression over three years compared with sleep education (12.2% vs 25.9%, HR 0.51) ([Irwin et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.3422)).
- Sleep hygiene advice alone is not an effective insomnia treatment. The AASM guideline strongly recommends multicomponent CBT-I and suggests against sleep hygiene as a stand-alone therapy ([Edinger et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.8986)).

### Values compass

- Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), which uses values-based action as a core element, reduced depression compared with control conditions in 18 studies (1,088 participants, SMD = 0.59). Support was strongest for mild depression and adults. Most studies had unclear randomisation and allocation methods ([Bai et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.09.040)).
- Values clarification on its own has mixed effects. A review of 50 RCTs (12,634 participants) found it improved positive mood, meaning, relatedness and valued living. Depression and anxiety were among the outcomes least likely to improve. Benefits were more consistent when people were encouraged to act on their values or set goals that fit them. Evidence quality was low to moderate ([Napier & Mangat, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2026.2714053)).

## Implications for Unpause

**Templates**

- Make the lowest template very low. Light activity and low weekly volume still bring benefit ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847); [Pearce et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0609)). People with more severe symptoms drop out more often ([Stubbs et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.10.019)) and find effort costlier ([Horne et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102065)). "Basic Sport" should be a realistic entry point, not a stepping stone the user is pushed past.
- Offer walking, yoga and simple strength exercises as the core sport options. They have the best evidence, and yoga and strength had the lowest dropout ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)).
- Do not frame "More Sport" as the goal. Benefits flatten above moderate volumes ([Pearce et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0609)). Suggest moving up only after a stretch of consistent weeks, and let the user decide.
- Give clear, concrete templates rather than an empty planner, but let users choose between options. Self-chosen habits are stronger ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)). The finding that more autonomy went with weaker effects ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)) is a reason to keep structure, not to remove choice.
- Connect sport templates to the Buddy feature (doing tasks together) and to local group options. Supervised programmes showed large effects and less dropout, and group-based exercise is what the authors recommend ([Heissel et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106282); [Stubbs et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.10.019)).

**Energy check-in**

- Use the answer to scale the day, not to cancel it. On a low-energy day, suggest one task, ideally light movement, with its two-minute version shown first. Activity tends to lift mood afterwards, especially in depression ([Difrancesco et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.23298)).
- Treat low energy as a symptom, not a lack of willpower. Fatigue is near universal in depression ([Ghanean et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-018-0490-z)). Copy should never imply laziness.
- Keep it to one tap per day, in line with the "less screen time" principle. Store energy next to the existing before-and-after mood ratings so users can see their own pattern. Person-level patterns differ a lot ([Difrancesco et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.23298)).
- If energy stays low for weeks, show a gentle prompt to talk to a doctor or therapist. Fatigue in depression can need its own treatment ([Ghanean et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40263-018-0490-z)).

**Two-minute version**

- Write each task and its two-minute version as an if-then plan tied to a cue, for example "After breakfast, I put on my shoes." If-then plans help people start, including people with mental health problems ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1); [Toli et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjc.12086)).
- Let users edit the cue and the tiny step themselves ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)).
- Count the two-minute version as a full success in points and reviews. This keeps the effort cost of starting low ([Horne et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102065)).
- Keep tasks at the same time and place each day. Tell users habits usually take two months or more and vary widely, and drop any "21 days" message ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674); [Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488)).
- A missed day does not break a habit ([Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)). This supports the comeback bonus over streaks.

**Daily rhythm anchors**

- Make the fixed wake-up time the main anchor and track how consistent it is, not how long the user sleeps. Regular wake times went with better next-day mood ([Fang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00400-z); [Lyall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(18)30139-1)). Allow a tolerance window so a late morning is not a failure.
- Offer a morning "go outside" task, which can double as a light-activity task. Present it as a helpful habit, not as light therapy. The outdoor-light evidence is observational ([Burns et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.08.056)). The light therapy trials used 10,000 lux boxes, which an app cannot replicate ([Menegaz de Almeida et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.2871)).
- Do not recommend light boxes on the app's own initiative. If mentioned at all, say they should be discussed with a clinician, because side effects were not analysed and people with bipolar disorder were mixed into the trials ([Menegaz de Almeida et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.2871)).
- Keep the evening wind-down reminder simple and do not present it as insomnia treatment, because sleep hygiene alone is not effective for that ([Edinger et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.8986)).
- If a user reports ongoing sleep problems, point them to CBT-I, including evidence-based digital programmes, or to their doctor. CBT-I improves depressive symptoms and may prevent new episodes ([Lee et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00800-3); [Irwin et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.3422)). Do not build a home-made version of CBT-I components into Unpause.

**Values compass**

- Every value the user picks should lead to at least one concrete task in the plan. Values work helps more consistently when people act on their values or set goals that fit them ([Napier & Mangat, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2026.2714053)).
- Keep the values step short and editable, and do not claim that choosing values reduces depression. That effect is inconsistent ([Napier & Mangat, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2026.2714053)). ACT as a whole has better support ([Bai et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.09.040)), but Unpause delivers only a small part of it.
- Use values in the weekly review ("this week you did 3 things for friendship"). This makes progress meaningful without adding new screens.

## Evidence gaps and risks

- **Exercise trials are weak in detail.** Most trials are small, unblinded and at high or unclear risk of bias, with signs of publication bias ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847); [Heissel et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-106282)). The best effects come from supervised or group programmes, and self-guided, app-prompted exercise may do less.
- **Dose is unclear.** The effect of total weekly volume was uncertain in the network meta-analysis ([Noetel et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847)). The dose-response data come from prevention cohorts, not treatment ([Pearce et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.0609)).
- **Energy-adaptive planning is untested.** No trial found here shows that adapting a plan to a morning energy rating improves outcomes. It could also backfire if "low" days are always made very easy and activity never grows.
- **The two-minute version is untested.** The evidence comes from if-then planning and habit studies, mostly in people without depression. The habit studies are small and often at high risk of bias ([Singh et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12232488); [Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- **Rhythm evidence is mostly observational.** Lyall and Burns are UK Biobank data from middle-aged and older adults. Fang studied young doctors under heavy work stress. Low mood may disrupt rhythms rather than the other way round ([Lyall et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(18)30139-1); [Burns et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2021.08.056); [Fang et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00400-z)).
- **Light therapy evidence does not transfer to "go outside."** The trials used light boxes as an add-on to treatment, did not report side effects, and mixed unipolar and bipolar patients ([Menegaz de Almeida et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.2871)).
- **dCBT-I is uneven.** Heterogeneity was high, most samples had mild symptoms, and in studies that reported completion, about 40% did not finish the programmes ([Lee et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00800-3)). The prevention result comes from one trial in older adults ([Irwin et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.3422)).
- **ACT and values evidence is modest.** ACT trials are small with unclear risk of bias, and searches were limited to English and Chinese publications ([Bai et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.09.040)). Values clarification alone did not reliably reduce depression ([Napier & Mangat, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2026.2714053)).
- **Possible harms:**
  - Templates and "moving up" can turn into pressure. Unfinished plans can feed self-blame, which is common in depression.
  - People with physical health conditions may need medical advice before harder exercise. None of the reviews here report adverse events in detail.
  - A fixed wake time and wind-down reminders may clash with shift work or with sleep advice from the user's own clinician.
  - Each extra check-in or reminder adds screen time and notifications, which goes against the app's own goal.
  - Higher symptom severity predicts dropout ([Stubbs et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2015.10.019)). The people who most need the plan may disengage first, so the app must not replace professional treatment for moderate to severe depression ([NICE, 2022](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)).
