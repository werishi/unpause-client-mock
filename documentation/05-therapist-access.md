# Therapist Access

> Covers README section: 5. Therapist access

## Summary

Sharing progress data and questionnaire scores with a therapist (routine outcome monitoring) has a small and inconsistent effect on its own. One large meta-analysis finds a small benefit, but a Cochrane review and a recent UK trial of PHQ-9 monitoring in depression find no clear improvement. The stronger argument for a therapist-connected mode comes from a different angle. Digital tools for depression work better with human contact, blended care (app plus face-to-face therapy) did as well as face-to-face care in a large European trial, and the German depression guideline says digital interventions should be accompanied and monitored by a clinician. For Unpause this means: build therapist access as support for the therapy conversation, not as a treatment in itself. Use the PHQ-9 correctly, with a safe response path for item 9 (suicidal thoughts). Make all sharing explicit, granular and easy to revoke, as GDPR and the German DiGA rules require.

## Key findings

### Progress feedback to therapists (routine outcome monitoring)

- Across 58 randomised and non-randomised studies (21,699 patients), giving therapists feedback on patient progress led to slightly better symptom reduction than no feedback (d = 0.15, 95% CI 0.10 to 0.20). The effect was similar for patients who were "not on track" (d = 0.17). Dropout was also slightly lower (OR 1.19, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.38) ([de Jong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102002)).
- A Cochrane review of 17 RCTs (8,787 participants) in common mental health disorders found no significant difference in symptoms between feedback and no-feedback groups (SMD -0.07, 95% CI -0.16 to 0.01; 12 studies). It also found no difference in the number of sessions. All evidence was rated low quality because of high risk of bias, and the authors found the evidence insufficient to support routine use ([Kendrick et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011119.pub2)).
- The PROMDEP cluster RCT tested PHQ-9 monitoring with feedback for people with depression in UK primary care. It found no improvement in depression at 12 weeks (BDI-II difference -0.46, 95% CI -2.16 to 1.26) and no evidence of better depression management. Quality of life was slightly better at 26 weeks. The authors suggest this may be because feedback made patients more aware of their own improvement ([Kendrick et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3310/plrq4216)).
- The German depression guideline (NVL) says monitoring is often not systematic enough. It recommends that monitoring should cover symptoms including suicidality, side effects, psychosocial status and adherence (recommendation 2-12). The starting point should be documented, ideally with validated instruments (2-13). Suggested intervals are weekly in the first 4 weeks, every 2 to 4 weeks in months 2 and 3, and longer after that (2-14). The guideline adds that 14-day intervals are reasonable in milder cases, that monitoring can be done by phone or electronically, and that monitoring too often can feel negative to patients ([NVL Unipolare Depression, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).

### The PHQ-9 as the check-in instrument

- In an individual participant data meta-analysis (29 studies, 6,725 participants compared against semi-structured interviews), a PHQ-9 cut-off of 10 or above gave a sensitivity of 0.88 and a specificity of 0.85 for major depression. This held regardless of age ([Levis et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l1476)). The PHQ-9 is therefore a good screening and severity tool, but not a diagnosis.
- In 434 older adults treated for depression in the IMPACT trial, the PHQ-9 was at least as responsive to change as a longer scale (SCL-20). Test-retest reliability was excellent. The minimal clinically important difference for an individual was 5 points on the 0 to 27 scale ([Löwe et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005650-200412000-00006)).
- The German guideline describes the PHQ-9 as valid, sensitive to change, quick to use in routine care and free of licence fees ([NVL Unipolare Depression, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).
- Terms of use: the official PHQ instruction manual states that the PHQ measures are in the public domain and that no permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute them. The measures were developed by Spitzer, Williams, Kroenke and colleagues with an educational grant from Pfizer, and translations (including German) are free to download from the PHQ website. The website also links to Pfizer's general terms of use ([PHQ Screeners, n.d.](https://www.phqscreeners.com/images/sites/g/files/g10016261/f/201412/instructions.pdf)).
- The PHQ-9 asks about the "last 2 weeks", so a two-week check-in matches its recall period. The manual gives severity bands of 0 to 4 (none or minimal), 5 to 9 (mild), 10 to 14 (moderate), 15 to 19 (moderately severe) and 20 to 27 (severe) ([PHQ Screeners, n.d.](https://www.phqscreeners.com/images/sites/g/files/g10016261/f/201412/instructions.pdf)).
- On item 9, the manual says that a final decision about the actual risk of self-harm requires a clinical interview ([PHQ Screeners, n.d.](https://www.phqscreeners.com/images/sites/g/files/g10016261/f/201412/instructions.pdf)). It also lists the PHQ-8, which is the PHQ-9 without item 9, uses the same cut-offs and is mainly used in research outside depression care.
- In a US health system (84,418 outpatients, 207,265 questionnaires), item 9 answers predicted later suicide attempts and deaths. One-year attempt risk rose from 0.4% ("not at all") to 4% ("nearly every day"), and suicide death risk from 0.03% to 0.3%. Item 9 stayed a strong predictor after adjusting for overall depression severity. The authors describe suicidal ideation as an enduring vulnerability, not only a short-term crisis ([Simon et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ps.201200587)).
- Against a structured suicide risk interview (C-SSRS) in 841 outpatients, 41.1% answered item 9 positively, but only 13.4% were at risk by the C-SSRS. Item 9 had a sensitivity of 87.6%, a specificity of 66.1%, a positive predictive value of 28.6% and a negative predictive value of 97.2%. The authors conclude that item 9 alone is not sufficient to assess suicide risk ([Na et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2018.02.045)).

### Human guidance, blended care and the therapeutic alliance

- The German guideline makes a strong recommendation that internet and mobile interventions should be accompanied by a therapist (4-2). The evidence is rated low quality, but the guideline sees potential harm when patients use these tools alone: no proper monitoring, being left alone with ups and downs, and worse symptoms from frustration and self-blame when nothing improves. It also says adherence and effectiveness should be monitored regularly (4-3) ([NVL Unipolare Depression, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).
- An individual patient data network meta-analysis (39 RCTs, 9,751 participants) found that both guided and unguided internet CBT beat control conditions. Guided programmes were better than unguided at the end of treatment (PHQ-9 difference -0.8 points, 95% CI -1.4 to -0.2), but not at 6 or 12 months. Guidance mainly helped people with PHQ-9 scores above 9, while differences were small in mild (subthreshold) depression ([Karyotaki et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364)).
- A pre-registered meta-analysis of 56 RCTs (13,335 patients) of internet interventions for depression found that more human contact predicted better outcomes and better adherence. Only interventions with contact both before and during the programme clearly beat interventions with no contact. Heterogeneity was high, and about 79% of trials had some concerns or a high risk of bias ([Krieger et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2023.100617)).
- In the E-COMPARED RCT (835 patients with major depression in routine care in nine European countries), blended CBT was non-inferior to face-to-face treatment as usual at 3, 6 and 12 months. It also led to lower odds of a depressive episode at 12 months (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.99). Deterioration was under 5% in both groups, and the working alliance was strong in both. Blended care was not cost-effective at 12 months from a health care perspective, because costs were not significantly lower ([Riper et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/72866)).
- Across 295 studies (more than 30,000 patients), a better therapeutic alliance was linked to better outcomes (r = 0.28). The link was about the same in internet-based therapy (r = 0.275, 23 studies) ([Flückiger et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/pst0000172)).

### Therapist homework

- In a meta-analysis of 23 studies (2,183 people), doing more therapy homework was linked to better outcomes, with a small to medium effect (r = 0.26, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.33). The size of the link depended on who rated the homework and how ([Mausbach et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-010-9297-z)).
- A meta-analysis of 17 CBT studies (2,312 clients) looked at both how much homework was done (quantity) and how well it was done (quality). Quantity was linked to outcome at the end of treatment (g = 0.79, 15 studies) and at follow-up (g = 0.51). Quality showed similar or larger links (g = 0.78 at the end of treatment, g = 1.07 at follow-up), but this rests on only 3 studies with very wide confidence intervals ([Kazantzis et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2016.05.002)).
- Both meta-analyses are correlational. They show that people who do more homework tend to improve more, not that assigning more homework causes improvement.

### Data protection and German regulation

- Under the GDPR, "data concerning health" includes personal data about a person's mental health (Article 4(15)). PHQ-9 answers, mood ratings and therapy-related task data fall under this. Processing health data is prohibited by default (Article 9(1)). One exception is explicit consent for one or more specified purposes (Article 9(2)(a)) ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).
- Consent can be withdrawn at any time, and withdrawing must be as easy as giving it (Article 7(3)). Consent is not freely given if a service is made conditional on consent to processing that the service does not need (Article 7(4)). Other relevant duties are data protection by design and by default (Article 25), the right to receive one's own data (data portability, Article 20), security measures such as pseudonymisation and encryption (Article 32), and a data protection impact assessment for large-scale processing of health data (Article 35(3)(b)) ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).
- The BfArM DiGA guide (version 3.6, December 2025) defines a DiGA as a CE-marked medical device of risk class I, IIa or IIb whose main function is digital. It must be used by the patient alone or by the patient and a care provider together. Tools used only by providers are not DiGA. A DiGA must give individual feedback based on the patient's input, and data for doctors must be prepared for them ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)).
- If data or reports are made available to care providers, the manufacturer must confirm that this happens under the patient's control and in line with data protection and security rules. A human-readable export that the patient hands over on a secure route meets this. A paper printout handed over by the patient counts as secure ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)).
- Other DiGA rules from the same guide: explicit, informed and voluntary consent before personal data is collected through the app. Processing is limited to specific purposes, and broad tracking of user activity is not allowed. Data may only be processed in Germany, the EU, the EEA, Switzerland or countries with an EU adequacy decision. Push notifications must not contain health data. Since 2025, a data security certificate under BSI TR-03161 is required. A positive care effect must be shown in a comparative study, either a medical benefit or a patient-relevant improvement in structure and process of care. Provisional listing allows a trial period of up to 12 months, which can be extended once by up to 12 months ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)).

## Implications for Unpause

1. **Frame therapist access as support for the conversation, not as a treatment.** Feedback to clinicians has small or unclear effects on depression outcomes ([de Jong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102002); [Kendrick et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011119.pub2); [Kendrick et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3310/plrq4216)). Do not claim that sharing data improves recovery. The value is that the therapist sees what happened between sessions, which is what the guideline asks for ([NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).
2. **Set up therapist access together in a session.** Human contact before and during a digital programme gave the best results ([Krieger et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2023.100617)), and the guideline wants digital tools to be accompanied by a therapist ([NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)). Offer a short joint setup flow: link the therapist, choose what is shared, set the check-in rhythm, agree how item 9 answers are handled, and add the first homework task. Where possible, build the safety plan in the same session (see [06-safety-plan.md](06-safety-plan.md)).
3. **Use the PHQ-9 every two weeks by default, but let the therapist and user adjust it together.** Two weeks matches the PHQ-9 recall period and the guideline's intervals ([PHQ Screeners, n.d.](https://www.phqscreeners.com/images/sites/g/files/g10016261/f/201412/instructions.pdf); [NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)). Allow weekly check-ins early in treatment if both agree. Do not ask more often, since frequent monitoring can feel negative. Record a baseline at linking. Use the official German translation from the PHQ website and show the developer attribution. Licence fees are not needed.
4. **Show the trend in a way that respects the instrument's limits.** For the therapist, show the total score over time with the standard severity bands. Mark changes of 5 or more points as meaningful and treat smaller changes as possible noise ([Löwe et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005650-200412000-00006)). Never present a score as a diagnosis, because about 15% of people without major depression score 10 or above ([Levis et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l1476)). Let users see their own trend if they want to, in gentle wording. PROMDEP hints that patients may benefit from seeing their own progress ([Kendrick et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3310/plrq4216)), which also fits the "look how far you've come" idea.
5. **Treat item 9 as a safety-critical path.**
   - Any answer above "not at all" should at once show a calm message with one-tap access to the safety plan and crisis lines. This must work whether or not a therapist is linked.
   - Tell the user plainly whether and when the therapist will see the answer. Never suggest that someone is watching in real time.
   - On the therapist view, show item 9 separately and clearly, with its history, not only as part of the total. The risk it signals is lasting, not only a short-term crisis ([Simon et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ps.201200587)).
   - Do not let the app score or label suicide risk. Most positive item 9 answers are not high risk on a structured interview, but some are, and only a clinical interview can decide ([Na et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2018.02.045); [PHQ Screeners, n.d.](https://www.phqscreeners.com/images/sites/g/files/g10016261/f/201412/instructions.pdf)).
   - When linking, the therapist should confirm how they will respond (for example, raise it at the next session, or contact the user sooner under their own practice protocol).
   - For users without a therapist, the team must decide with clinical advisers whether to use the PHQ-9 with the crisis path or the PHQ-8 (no item 9). This is an open design decision, not something the evidence settles.
6. **Design therapist homework around a few clear tasks and how they went.** Homework completion is linked to better outcomes, and how well a task is done may matter as much as whether it is done ([Mausbach et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-010-9297-z); [Kazantzis et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2016.05.002)). Let therapists add a small number of concrete tasks, each with a two-minute version. Record not just "done" but also a short "how did it go" and the mood before and after, so the next session can discuss quality. Users should be able to adjust or decline a task, and missed tasks should never cost points.
7. **Keep the session summary factual and identical for both sides.** No study tested automatic "since our last session" summaries. Base the content on what the guideline says monitoring should cover: PHQ-9 trend and change since the last session, item 9 answers, homework done and how it went, routines, energy and mood ratings, and anything the user wants to raise ([NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)). Highlight worsening, in line with the "not on track" idea in feedback research ([de Jong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102002)). Avoid interpretive text. Let the user see the summary before the session and add a note such as "what I want to talk about".
8. **Make sharing off by default, granular and easy to revoke.** Health data needs explicit consent for specified purposes, and withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). Ask for consent separately for each data type (PHQ-9 total, item 9, routines, points, mood ratings, notes) and for each therapist. Revoking should take one tap and take effect at once. All other features must work without a therapist, because consent must not be a condition for unrelated parts of the service (Article 7(4)). Add a "see what my therapist sees" preview and a log of when shared data was viewed. Offer a human-readable export or printout, which matches both GDPR data portability and the DiGA route for sharing data with providers ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)).
9. **Share only what the therapy needs.** Data protection by default means only the data needed for each purpose ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)), and DiGA rules forbid broad tracking of user activity ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)). For example, media use details and the content of interest searches should not reach the therapist by default. Points are better shown as optional context than as a measure of effort, so the therapist view does not turn into a compliance score.
10. **Plan hosting and security for health data from the start.** Host in the EU/EEA, encrypt data, pseudonymise where possible, and do a data protection impact assessment before launch ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). Notifications such as "Your therapist added a task" are fine, but never put scores or item 9 answers into push notifications ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)).
11. **Decide the regulatory path early.** Monitoring depression with a validated questionnaire and sharing it with a therapist moves Unpause towards a medical purpose. A DiGA must be a CE-marked medical device, hold a BSI TR-03161 certificate and prove a positive care effect in a comparative study ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)). The team should get regulatory advice on the intended purpose before building the therapist features out fully.

## Evidence gaps and risks

- **Feedback effects are small and mixed.** The positive meta-analysis includes non-randomised studies and finds only a small effect ([de Jong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102002)). The Cochrane review rates all evidence as low quality ([Kendrick et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD011119.pub2)). The trial closest to Unpause's use case (PHQ-9 monitoring in depression) found no benefit on depression ([Kendrick et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3310/plrq4216)). Most feedback research uses psychotherapy-specific systems, not an app that shares routines and points.
- **The specific features are untested.** This review found no trials of therapist dashboards showing gamification points or routines, of therapist-assigned tasks inside a self-help app, or of automatic session summaries. These are design choices built on indirect evidence.
- **Homework evidence is correlational.** People who are already improving may simply do more homework. The quality findings rest on only 3 studies ([Kazantzis et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beth.2016.05.002)).
- **Blended care evidence comes from structured CBT programmes.** Unpause is a planning and activation tool, not a full CBT programme, so the E-COMPARED results may not transfer. Blended care was also not cost-effective at 12 months ([Riper et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/72866)).
- **The benefit of guidance fades.** Guided programmes beat unguided ones at the end of treatment, but not at 6 or 12 months, and the gain was mainly in moderate or worse depression ([Karyotaki et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364)). Many guidance trials have risk-of-bias concerns ([Krieger et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.invent.2023.100617)). The guideline's strong recommendation rests on low-quality evidence plus clinical judgement ([NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).
- **PHQ-9 evidence has limits.** The 5-point threshold comes from older US adults in collaborative care ([Löwe et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005650-200412000-00006)). The accuracy data come from screening settings, not from repeated use in an app ([Levis et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.l1476)). Answering the same questions every two weeks may lead to fatigue or habitual answers.
- **Item 9 is hard to handle well.** It produces many false alarms ([Na et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2018.02.045)), which can burden therapists and worry users. It also signals real and lasting risk ([Simon et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ps.201200587)), so ignoring it is not an option. This review did not find peer-reviewed evaluations of specific in-app alert workflows for item 9. The team needs clinical and legal advice on who is responsible when an answer is given but not seen.
- **Possible harms:**
  - Feeling watched, or performing for the therapist instead of using the app honestly.
  - Shame when low activity or worsening scores become visible, which conflicts with "Encouraging, never shaming".
  - Pressure from therapist tasks on low-energy days.
  - Consent that is not truly free, because the therapist suggested the app and the user may not want to say no.
  - False reassurance that someone is monitoring in real time.
  - Too frequent check-ins that feel like a burden ([NVL, 2022](https://www.leitlinien.de/themen/depression/langfassung/depression-vers3-2.pdf)).
  - Leaks or misuse of very sensitive mental health data.
  - Therapists making decisions from scores without a clinical conversation.
- **Regulation is still changing.** The DiGA guide says the planned data protection certification under § 139e(11) SGB V is not yet in operation, and that BfArM's view on data processing abroad does not bind data protection authorities ([BfArM, 2025](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Medizinprodukte/diga_leitfaden.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)). Rules should be re-checked before any submission.
