# Behavioral Activation

> Covers README section: Foundation: behavioral activation

## Summary

Behavioral activation (BA) is one of the best-supported psychological treatments for depression. Meta-analyses and a Cochrane review find it clearly better than control conditions and about as effective as cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), and large trials show that non-specialists can deliver it well. Digital BA also reduces depressive symptoms, but the effects are smaller, tend to fade after about six months, look weaker without any human guidance, and most people stop using mental health apps early. For Unpause this supports activity scheduling as the core of the app. The before-and-after mood rating fits BA theory and has some indirect support, but no trial has tested it as a separate ingredient, so it should be kept light and framed honestly.

## Key findings

### Efficacy of behavioral activation

- An early meta-analysis of 16 studies (780 participants) found a large effect of activity scheduling compared with control conditions at post-test (effect size 0.87, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.15). The difference from other psychological treatments was small and not significant (0.13), and benefits held at follow-up ([Cuijpers et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2006.11.001)).
- An updated meta-analysis of 26 RCTs (1,524 participants) found BA better than controls (SMD -0.74, 25 trials) and better than antidepressant medication (SMD -0.42, only 4 trials). There was no sign of publication bias, but study quality was low in most trials and follow-up was short ([Ekers et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100100)).
- The Cochrane review (53 RCTs, 5,495 adults with clinical or subthreshold depression) found BA more effective than treatment as usual in the short term (RR 1.40, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.78; moderate certainty). This advantage disappeared in worst-case and intention-to-treat sensitivity analyses. BA and CBT showed no difference (RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.07; 5 RCTs, 601 participants; moderate certainty). Most findings were rated moderate certainty or lower, and dropout did not clearly differ from most comparators ([Uphoff et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013305.pub2)).
- In the COBRA trial, 440 adults with major depression received either BA from junior mental health workers or CBT from experienced therapists. At 12 months both groups had the same mean PHQ-9 score (8.4; difference 0.1, 95% CI -1.3 to 1.5), so BA was non-inferior to CBT. BA needs less intensive and less costly training. Only 61% of the BA group attended at least eight sessions ([Richards et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0)).
- In India, 495 adults with moderately severe to severe depression were randomized to enhanced usual care with or without the Healthy Activity Program, a BA-based treatment of 6 to 8 sessions delivered by lay counsellors. At 3 months, 64% in the BA group were in remission versus 39% with usual care alone (adjusted prevalence ratio 1.61). The BA group also had fewer suicidal thoughts or attempts. The program was cost-effective, and serious adverse events were rare and similar in both groups (4% each) ([Patel et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31589-6)).
- The UK NICE guideline lists group and individual BA among the first-line psychological treatments for less severe depression. The committee judged group CBT and group BA likely to be the most cost-effective options for this group. Individual BA is also listed among the options for more severe depression ([NICE NG222, 2022](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222)).

### Activity, pleasure and mood: the logic behind before-and-after ratings

- In a smartphone study of 240 patients in blended CBT, weekly ratings of pleasant activities, pleasure and mood were positively linked in the same week, and all three rose slightly during treatment. However, more activity in one week did not predict better mood the next week. The authors say causality cannot be inferred and that daily rather than weekly ratings may be needed to see these effects ([van Genugten et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32007)).
- In an experience-sampling study (35 people with major depression, 26 with minor depression, 38 healthy controls), depressed people rated daily events as less pleasant. Yet after positive events, their negative mood dropped more than in controls (a "mood-brightening" effect). This supports the idea that doing something positive can lift mood in depression, and that a before-and-after rating can capture it. It is a small observational study, not a test of an intervention ([Bylsma et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021662)).
- A meta-analysis of 18 RCTs of depression apps (3,336 participants) found that dropout was lower in studies whose apps included in-app mood monitoring or human feedback ([Torous et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167)). This is an association across studies, not proof that mood ratings cause better engagement.
- We found no trial that compares BA with and without per-activity mood ratings, so the specific value of this feature is untested.

### Digital and smartphone-delivered behavioral activation

- A meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (3,274 participants, 88% women) found that internet-based BA reduced depressive symptoms compared with inactive controls at post-treatment (Hedges g -0.49, 95% CI -0.63 to -0.34). The effect was not significant at 6 months (only 2 studies). Guided and minimally guided programs both worked (SMD -0.50 each), but the 3 unguided programs did not reach significance. Browser-based programs showed slightly larger effects than smartphone apps (-0.54 vs -0.39). All studies had at least some risk of bias ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643)).
- A broader meta-analysis of 17 RCTs of digital BA (web or app, messaging, and telehealth) found significant reductions in depression at 2, 3 and 6 months, but not at 12 months. Quality of life improved. There was no significant effect on anxiety or on functioning. Most programs (12 of 17) were guided ([Jia et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)).
- In a large RCT, 649 primary care patients with at least moderate depression (PHQ-9 of 10 or more) used a self-directed BA app (Moodivate) or got usual care for 12 weeks. App users improved more on the BDI-II (mean change -10.34 vs -5.94) and were more likely to improve meaningfully (OR 2.98) and to reach remission (OR 2.27). Only 33% were still using the app at 12 weeks, and only 14% of doctors used the option to see app data ([Dahne et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.0494)).
- A component network meta-analysis of 76 RCTs of internet CBT for depression (individual data from 11,704 participants) found suggestive evidence that a BA component helps (about 1.8 PHQ-9 points better) and that a relaxation component might be harmful (about 1.2 points worse). Combining human and automated encouragement reduced dropout (incremental OR 0.32) ([Furukawa et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00077-8)).
- An individual participant data meta-analysis of internet CBT (39 trials, 9,751 participants) found that guided programs beat unguided ones at post-treatment by a small margin (0.8 PHQ-9 points), with no difference at 6 or 12 months. Guidance mattered little for subthreshold depression (PHQ-9 5 to 9) and more when PHQ-9 was above 9 ([Karyotaki et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364)). This is CBT rather than BA, but the guidance question is the same.

### Engagement, adherence and dropout

- In trials of depression apps, pooled dropout was 26.2%, rising to 47.8% after adjusting for publication bias. Larger studies had higher dropout ([Torous et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167)).
- Real-world use is far lower than in trials. Across 93 popular Android mental health apps, median retention was 3.9% after 15 days and 3.3% after 30 days. Tracker apps kept somewhat more users than breathing-exercise apps ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567)).
- Even in a successful trial, use fell off: weekly retention was 68% to 100% in the first month, but only a third of users were still active at 12 weeks ([Dahne et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.0494)).

### Safety

- In an individual participant data meta-analysis of self-guided internet CBT (13 RCTs, 3,805 adults), 5.8% of the intervention group and 9.1% of the control group showed clinically significant deterioration (OR 0.62). Self-guided help did not increase deterioration, but a minority of users still got worse ([Karyotaki et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291718000648)).
- In the face-to-face trials, serious adverse events were rare and not linked to BA ([Richards et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0); [Patel et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31589-6)).

## Implications for Unpause

1. **Make activity scheduling the core loop.** Plan a small activity, do it, rate it. This is the part of BA with the strongest evidence ([Cuijpers et al., 2007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2006.11.001); [Ekers et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100100)), and the BA component also stands out within internet CBT ([Furukawa et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00077-8)). Other features (media budget, digest, points) should serve this loop, not compete with it.
2. **Keep the method simple.** BA works when delivered by junior workers and lay counsellors ([Richards et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0); [Patel et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31589-6)). That supports a plain-language, self-help format. But those people were trained and supervised, so Unpause should not claim to replace therapy.
3. **Keep the before-and-after mood rating, but keep it light.** Use one quick tap (for example a 5-point or 0 to 10 scale). The idea is plausible: activity and mood move together ([van Genugten et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32007)), positive events lift mood in depression ([Bylsma et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021662)), and apps with mood monitoring have lower dropout ([Torous et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167)). But it is not proven as a separate ingredient.
4. **Do not promise that every task lifts mood.** Depressed people often rate events as less pleasant ([Bylsma et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021662)), and week-to-week links are weak ([van Genugten et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32007)). Show averages and trends over days and weeks rather than a verdict on one task. Treat "no change" as normal, not as failure.
5. **Use the ratings to personalize.** Show users which kinds of activities were most often followed by a better mood, and suggest more of those. Consider an optional "enjoyment" rating next to mood, since pleasure is one of the three BA constructs ([van Genugten et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32007)).
6. **Offer human support, especially for moderate to severe symptoms.** Unguided internet BA did not reach significance ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643)). Guidance helps more when PHQ-9 is above 9 ([Karyotaki et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364)). Human feedback and human plus automated encouragement are linked to lower dropout ([Torous et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167); [Furukawa et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00077-8)). The therapist link, buddy feature and gentle reminders all fit this. A self-directed app can still work ([Dahne et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.0494)), so the solo mode is worth keeping.
7. **Design for drop-off from day one.** Expect most users to fade within weeks ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567); [Dahne et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.0494)). Keep each check-in very short, use a small number of well-timed reminders, and make returning easy (the comeback bonus fits this).
8. **Plan for the long run.** Digital BA effects fade by 6 to 12 months ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643); [Jia et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)). Monthly reviews, booster plans and "what helped me" summaries can support maintenance, though this is untested.
9. **Do not pad the program.** More modules are not automatically better. Relaxation as a component may even worsen outcomes in internet CBT ([Furukawa et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00077-8)). Add extra content only when there is evidence for it.
10. **Measure what the trials measure, and watch for worsening.** Use a validated scale such as the PHQ-9 for trends. Some users get worse even with help ([Karyotaki et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291718000648)), so a rising score should lead to a clear prompt to seek professional help and to the safety plan.

## Evidence gaps and risks

- **Trial quality.** Many BA trials are small, low quality and have short follow-up ([Ekers et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0100100)). The Cochrane review rates most findings moderate certainty or lower, and the advantage over usual care disappears in intention-to-treat analyses ([Uphoff et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013305.pub2)).
- **Durability.** Digital BA effects are not clearly maintained at 6 to 12 months, and long-term data come from very few studies ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643); [Jia et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/68054)).
- **Unguided use.** Evidence for fully self-guided BA is mixed: only 3 unguided programs in one meta-analysis, which did not reach significance ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643)), versus one large positive trial ([Dahne et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.0494)). That trial compared the app with usual care, not with an attention control, and was run in US primary care.
- **Mood ratings.** No trial tests per-activity before-and-after ratings as a separate feature. The supporting evidence is correlational ([van Genugten et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/32007)), observational and small ([Bylsma et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021662)), or a cross-study association ([Torous et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2019.11.167)). We found no direct evidence on whether repeated low ratings discourage some users. This should be watched in user testing.
- **Who was studied.** Trial samples are mostly women (88% in one meta-analysis) ([Alber et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.2196/41643)). The Cochrane review excluded inpatient studies ([Uphoff et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD013305.pub2)). The evidence does not cover people in acute crisis, and Unpause should not be the only support for someone with severe depression or suicidal thoughts.
- **Engagement.** Real-world engagement is far lower than in trials ([Baumel et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/14567)). Even face-to-face, many people stop early: only 61% of the BA group in COBRA attended at least eight sessions ([Richards et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0)). Trial effect sizes may not transfer to an app in daily life.
- **Possible harms.** A minority of users deteriorate during self-guided digital treatment, although less often than without it ([Karyotaki et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291718000648)). That evidence comes from internet CBT, not BA specifically. Other risks, such as pressure from task lists or shame after missed tasks, are not well studied for BA apps and need to be checked in testing.
- **Transfer from CBT.** Some digital findings used here (guidance, components, deterioration) come from internet CBT, where BA is one part, not from stand-alone BA ([Karyotaki et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2020.4364); [Furukawa et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2215-0366(21)00077-8)).
